## Did the debate find anything real?

**Yes, and one finding the baseline missed.**

**B1** — the Skeptic independently reproduced the baseline's #1, the strongest finding in
either arm:

> TASK-029 omits persistent inhibition of the installed interval-driven binary watchdog;
> its restart path bypasses the maintenance marker.

with actual verification, not assertion:

> `SERVICES_LONG_RUNNING: binary-watchdog=False, factory-watchdog=False;
> binary-watchdog: StartInterval=600, KeepAlive=False, maintenance references=0;
> 84: if FORCE=1 "$HOME/.multica/bin/multica-ctl" restart …;
> read(multica-ctl,201..220) shows load_one uses launchctl load -w`

Two independent models, different families, different tooling, same conclusion. That is
the corroboration the design was built for.

**B2 — a finding the baseline explicitly listed under "What I missed":**

> TASK-028 does not establish a writer barrier: an independent factory-watchdog sweep can
> mutate issues and rerun work after registered triggers are disabled.
>
> `factory-watchdog: StartInterval=900 … 122: multica --workspace-id "$ws" issue label
> add …; 135: multica … issue rerun "$id"`

The baseline wrote: *"I focused on `binary-watchdog` re-animating daemons but didn't fully
assess whether `factory-watchdog`'s escalation writes or `multica issue rerun` hints could
trigger writes post-quiesce."* The debate went and checked it. That is one genuine
marginal finding attributable to the protocol.

## The four fixes, each verified against measured behaviour

**§13.34 — `disputed` no longer closes the gate.** Probe 1's Skeptic parked its own three
high-severity claims as `disputed` and ended the debate at R2. The re-probe reached **R3**.
An unevidenced claim moved off `open` now still counts as unsettled.

**§13.35 — revised mid-implementation.** My first attempt *demoted* unevidenced
high/critical claims to medium. That was wrong and the e2e test caught it: demotion
removes the claim from the R3 gate, so the one mechanism that could produce the missing
verification never fires. Inflating severity only costs money (bounded by `rounds.max` and
the cost caps); parking claims hides risk. Now the severity stands and drives the gate,
while the claim is flagged `high_severity_unverified` everywhere it is reported.

**§13.36 — the Skeptic mission demands executed tests.** Evidence coverage went from ~0 to
**16/16**, tool calls 9 → 48. Still only 11 bash calls versus the baseline's 28 — it
prefers `read`/`grep` over shell, which is fine as long as the evidence is real, and it is.

**§13.37 — judge gets a computed evidence audit.** Instead of trusting it to audit 16
claims, `prompts.ts` now hands it orchestrator-computed counts and names the unevidenced
ids, with a hard instruction to cap confidence at 0.5 when most high-severity claims lack
evidence. Result: 0.8 justified by cited command output, rather than 0.9 justified by
nothing.

## Two further defects the re-probe exposed

**§13.39 — one author was silently erasing the other's findings.** In re-probe attempt 3
the Ideator rewrote **all three** of the Skeptic's high-severity claims, replacing the
findings with its own rebuttals ("Disputed severity: B1's operational risk depends on…")
and replacing the Skeptic's command output with seed quotes. §8.1 grants the Ideator
`text` and `evidence`, which is right for its own claims and destructive on someone
else's. The ledger then reported **100% evidence coverage while the actual verification
had been deleted** — the original survived only in the archived turn file. This is the
most dangerous class of bug found so far: it makes the output look *better* while
destroying the evidence. Fixed: `text`/`evidence`/`type`/`confidence` are own-claim-only;
disagreement goes through `status` + `refutedPremise`, which is what flip discipline is
for. Fixing it also revealed the Skeptic could never edit its own `text` (it wasn't in
`SKEPTIC_FIELDS`) while the Ideator could rewrite it freely — exactly the asymmetry that
enabled the erasure.

**§13.38 — failed turns were undiagnosable.** Attempt 2 lost 5 turns to transient
`stopReason: "error"`, and the cause was unrecoverable because the runner discarded
`stderrTail`. All three providers tested healthy minutes later, so it was a provider
blip — but I could only establish that by re-running. Now `stderrTail` and the provider
`errorMessage` are retained on failed turns in both the manifest and `events.jsonl`.

## Recommendation

The protocol now works as designed. Whether to keep it is a value judgement, and I'd frame
it honestly:

**For keeping it:** it independently corroborated the baseline's best finding via a
different model family, and found one thing the baseline missed. The ledger is a better
artifact than prose — sortable, citable, with per-claim evidence and status history. The
$5/run default cap comfortably covers a run like this.

**Against:** $2.11 vs $0.13 is 16.6× for one marginal finding on this seed. A single strong
model with bash and a self-critique pass got 4 verified findings for pocket change, and it
was honest about its own weak spots without being forced to be.

**My read:** the debate earns its cost when being wrong is expensive — an irreversible
migration, a production cutover — and does not when a quick review would do. That is a
per-invocation judgement, which argues for keeping it as an explicit `/debate` command
rather than something automatic. The `tier: "free"` path also exists for the
cost-sensitive case, at the price of D8 independence.

**Recommend proceeding to WP6**, with the caveat that WP8 should measure the full 51.8KB
document before this is called finished — every finding here is against a 6KB excerpt, and
both arms noted they were critiquing a truncated plan.
